About this atlas

Common questions

Are these official statistics?

Regional figures are drawn directly from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), while provincial figures are based on official estimates adjusted through smoothing. Estimates for barangays were produced independently for this project.

Why does the estimate for my barangay seem inaccurate?

The estimate may indeed be inaccurate. Margins of error are relatively wide, and the model relies on satellite imagery and a limited number of older surveys. If you identify an error, such as an incorrect name or geographic location, please report it.

Why does the estimated number of people living in poverty differ from the PSA's figure?

The PSA estimates that 11.08 million Filipinos live in poverty, using its own 2025 population base. This dataset applies poverty rates to the 2024 census population, resulting in an estimate of 10.91 million.

Why are some areas left blank?

Twelve barangays had no recorded residents in the 2024 census, while 21 geographic polygons correspond to forests, parks, or other areas that are not classified as barangays.

Report a problem

Use the GitHub issue tracker to report incorrect names, misplaced barangays, broken pages, or methodological errors. Any corrections will be documented in the version history.

Credits

Official statistics come from the Philippine Statistics Authority. Door-to-door barangay data comes from Community-Based Monitoring System reports published by the Partnership for Economic Policy and the City of Bacolod. Boundaries come from the PSGC shapefiles maintained by altcoder and faeldon. Satellite data comes from NOAA, NASA, ESA, the European Commission's Joint Research Centre, Google and the Malaria Atlas Project, accessed through Google Earth Engine. The income calculator uses income classes from the Philippine Institute for Development Studies and living wage figures from IBON Foundation.
