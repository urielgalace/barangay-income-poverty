How this is calculated

Income percentile

The Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES) 2025 reports average household income for ten income groups in each region, province, and highly urbanized city, ranked by per capita income. However, individual household records are not publicly available. FIES 2023 also provides the distribution of households by size and their corresponding average incomes. For each geographic area, a model of household income and household size is fitted to these figures, together with the official poverty rate where available.

Income percentiles are calculated by ranking individuals according to household income adjusted for household size. The adjustment uses the square-root equivalence scale, which divides household income by the square root of the number of household members. This accounts for economies of scale from sharing housing and other household expenses. Individual income is treated as the income of a single-person household. Reported income is converted to 2025 prices using the PSA’s consumer price index, assuming that incomes have increased in line with inflation since 2025.

For cities, municipalities, and barangays without separately published FIES figures, the model uses the income distribution of the corresponding province or region, adjusted to match the area’s estimated average household income after accounting for household size. This assumes that the underlying distribution of income is similar across geographic areas. Consequently, the resulting percentiles should be interpreted as estimates. For barangays, the reported ranges incorporate uncertainty in the estimated average income. Provincial figures exclude highly urbanized cities, consistent with FIES reporting conventions.

The models were evaluated by comparing their implied poverty rates with official figures before poverty rates were incorporated into the estimation procedure. Across 101 geographic areas with published poverty rates, the average absolute difference was 2.6 percentage points, falling to 1.8 percentage points for regions. At the national level, the model estimated a poverty rate of 10.6% in 2025, compared with the official figure of 9.7%. The same approach also reproduced the income-class shares published by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) for 2018, with deviations of no more than 1.3 percentage points for each class. However, because the PSA does not publish income distributions ranked by household-size-adjusted income, the percentile rankings themselves cannot be validated directly against official statistics.

Income class

Income classifications are based on the framework developed by the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (Albert, Santos, and Vizmanos, 2018), which defines income classes as multiples of the official poverty threshold.

The calculator uses the national poverty threshold of ₱35,121 per person annually in 2025, adjusted to September 2026 prices using the PSA’s consumer price index, which increased by 7.4% over the period. These thresholds are updated automatically each month following the release of new inflation figures by the PSA.

For a household of five, the resulting thresholds correspond directly to those defined by PIDS, with a monthly poverty threshold of ₱15,720. However, PIDS applies its classifications to per capita income, which would imply a poverty threshold for a single-person household equal to one-fifth of the threshold for a family of five.

Instead, this calculator adjusts income thresholds across household sizes using the square-root equivalence scale. Under this approach, the poverty threshold for a single-person household is ₱15,720 divided by the square root of five, or approximately ₱7,030 per month. Consequently, the income classifications for households with sizes other than five differ from those obtained by applying the original PIDS methodology.

Living wage

Living wage estimates are based on the family living wage figures published by the IBON Foundation in August 2026, both nationally and by region. These figures represent the income required for a family of five to maintain a decent standard of living. IBON derives its estimates using data from the National Wages and Productivity Commission and the PSA.

Daily regional living wage estimates are converted to monthly amounts assuming 261 paid working days annually. This procedure reproduces IBON’s published national monthly figure to within ₱8.

For households of other sizes, the living wage is adjusted using the same square-root equivalence scale applied to the income percentiles and income classifications. Accordingly, the living wage for a single-person household is calculated by dividing the figure for a family of five by the square root of five.

Because IBON does not publish living wage estimates below the regional level, cities, municipalities, and barangays are assigned the figure for their respective regions.

For Caraga, the national living wage is used because the commission did not publish a regional estimate. For the Negros Island Region, which also lacks a separate estimate, Negros Occidental is assigned the Western Visayas figure, while Negros Oriental and Siquijor are assigned the Central Visayas figure. The national estimate is used for the Negros Island Region as a whole.
